2nd ROSCon UK Education Workshop:
Bridging the gap between ROS education and industry requirements

Date & Time: 9:30 AM - 1:00 PM Thursday 10th October 2026

Venue: John McIntyre Conference Centre (JMCC)

Room: Pentland East

Overview

How do we teach ROS most effectively at an undergraduate level, and how do we ensure that graduates are leaving university with the right skills to "hit the ground running" in a robotics career?

This workshop will bring together ROS educators and industry experts to tackle the challenge of bridging these two domains. One of the outcomes of last year's Education Workshop was that a community should be established to share resources, approaches, best practices and industry requirements. A further aim of this workshop will therefore be to co-create an actionable roadmap, defining communication pipelines, and measurable milestones to sustainably grow this initiative and systematically close the industry-readiness gap.

Presenters

Presenters from both industry and academia will share their experiences, and discuss what they see as the current gaps between academic training and industry requirements.

Outline and Goals

This workshop addresses the disconnect between undergraduate robotics curricula and the industry-relevant skills required for a robotics career. Building directly on the momentum and feedback from last year's Education Workshop (see here), this session will aim to move from high-level discussion to tangible and sustained action.

Key Aims:

  1. Identify the major gaps: Pinpoint the top 5 technical and pedagogical discrepancies between academic outputs and industry hiring requirements.
  2. Strengthen the ROS UK Education Community: Formally establish a working group, the necessary governance and identify platforms to facilitate communication and sharing of resources.
  3. Create a Roadmap: With a key aim to maintain a high level of activity within the network, set out what we plan to achieve, and some specific milestones to deliver before ROSCon UK 2027.

Intended Audience and Learning Outcomes

This workshop is for:

  • Educators: University lecturers, module leaders, and researchers designing or teaching robotics courses.
  • Industry Experts: CTOs, engineering managers, and senior developers who hire graduates and understand day-to-day deployment challenges.
  • Learners: Anyone with an interest in learning ROS, or those with prior learning experiences (whether as part of a formal course or self-directed), and who may have a story to share, or a desire to learn more!

The above audiences can expect to take away from this workshop the following:

  • Educators: How to teach ROS effectively, and how to adapt modules to enhance the development of industry-relevant ROS (and related) skills.
  • Industry Experts: How to influence the approaches to teaching ROS at university level, to support recruitment and onboarding. Support the development of meaningful, and achievable teaching resources and teaching tools.
  • Learners: Access to or the ability to contribute to the development of shared open educational resources for learning ROS.
  • All: How to actively participate in and benefit from a newly structured, year-round UK ROS Education Network.

Workshop Schedule

Session Time Description
Welcome & Introductions 09:30 - 09:45 Introduction to the workshop, the agenda, and the key goals.
This will also include an introduction by Ingo Keller. Here, Ingo will set the scene; discussing the current mismatches between industry and academia, and why an active community is vital to bridging the gaps.
Lighting talks: "Perspectives from the Field" 09:45 - 10:45 Short talks by colleagues from both academia and industry (see the list of presenters above). These talks will highlight the realities and challenges faced on both sides, followed by an open Q&A.
Academic perspectives will include topics such as:
  • The world of ROS/Robotics moves fast. How do we keep up to date?
  • Approaches to teaching ROS at university level
  • Building modules and curriculums appropriately
Industry perspectives will include topics such as:
  • What actually are the "industry-relevant skills"
  • Graduate recruitment experiences
  • How to train graduates to "hit the ground running" in an industry role. How to support the Continued Professional Development (CPD) of employees.
Each talk will conclude by stating what specific gaps our speakers observe in their roles, and what they need from the community to close those gaps.
Identifying topics for further discussion 10:45 - 11:00 Having heard different perspectives from our presenters, and bringing in further opinions from attendees (as submitted in advance via a pre-workshop survey). Participants will then have the chance to vote on the topics that are most important to them. This voting will then be used to establish 3 or 4 key topics for in-depth discussion (in breakout groups) after the coffee break.
Coffee Break 11:00 - 11:30 Morning Tea & Coffee break for all conference attendees @ JMCC Centro.
Breakout group discussions 11:30 - 12:30 Having established the key topics for discussion, participants will then choose a breakout group to join, and discuss their chosen topic in more depth. Groups will be brought back together for the final 15 minutes of this section to present the outcomes.
Establishing a Roadmap & Next Steps 12:30 - 13:00 What will we aim to achieve between now and ROSCon UK 2027?
Here we will formalise some actionable milestones to be achieved throughout the next year and assign owners to these.
